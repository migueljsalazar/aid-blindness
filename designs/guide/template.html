03 · How we grade

Reachability, not worthiness

Our grades measure one thing: how likely a person in need is to actually get the help — how broad the coverage is, how much paperwork stands in the way, how fast the help arrives, and how reliably it's funded.

They are not a judgment of how worthy the organization is. A neighborhood food pantry earns an S because you can walk in today and leave with groceries. Section 8 sits at D — not because rent vouchers don't matter (they're life-changing) but because the waitlists run two to ten years. If we sent you there first, we'd be wasting the week you actually have.

Every entry cites its sources so you can check our work, and we take no referral fees from anyone we grade. When a program's rules change, the grade changes.
